How much can be mined from studying one’s digital footprint? With the European Union’s GDPR in place, I wonder how many internet users have actually reached out to request a copy of their data. As an experiment — since DoorDash falls under US regulations and not GDPR’s purview — I started collecting my receipts from the past year, and here are some of the cumulative findings.

Some of my most-visited restaurants are, well, shamefully within walking distance of my house. And although these are places I know well, I’ve actually set foot in no more than a handful of them over the past year.

Some of the places I’ve ordered from most frequently are Dim Sum Sam, a Cantonese restaurant, about 17 times, and Din Tai Fung, a Taiwanese-Shanghainese restaurant, 8 times.

Out of the 102 Manhattan delivery orders, there were 32 unique restaurants. Most restaurants clustered in the East Village and around the Nomad/Flatiron area.

As I investigated the ordering behaviors on the platform further, a phenomenon stood out. As time went on, I couldn’t help ordering more and more frequently from DoorDash — and ordering more food each time — all because of Double Dash. Double Dash, as the software company claims, “lets you add items from a second nearby store or restaurant to your original order within 15 to 20 minutes of checkout, with no extra delivery fee.” More than a fifth of my total orders were Double Dash.

So what do I spend Double Dashing on?

The thing is, the second store was never a small thing. In my head, it was “just grabbing something extra to nibble on.” On the receipt, it was on average $27 for each add-on order — two cream puffs at $13.75 apiece, or a box of cake rolls at $16 — while my actual dinner that night averaged around $40. So the little extra I tacked on was two-thirds the size of the meal I’d actually sat down to order, and about what a whole normal takeout order costs me on its own. Over the year, it added up to $486 — and I never put much thought into this line item.

The frequency of Double Dash orders increased significantly during the latter six months of the analysis. After taking a closer look, it appears I’d picked up a habit of ordering a plate of dessert. Among these shops, Lady M’s Amedeo chocolate mille-feuille cake was ordered 4 times, for a total of 7 pieces, and ando Patisserie’s Swiss cake roll was ordered 5 times, for a total of 6 pieces.
